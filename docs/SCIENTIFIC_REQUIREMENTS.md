## 1. Principles of Scientific Honesty & Data Contracts

In aerospace life support and spacecraft fire safety engineering, misrepresenting an exploratory hypothesis as an experimental law or claiming an uncalibrated model has "$\pm 14\%$ uncertainty" is unacceptable. GODEYE // PYRO-SI enforces a strict 6-tier scientific data contract for every value, chart, and statement in the application:

### 1.1. Six-Tier Provenance Classification
- **`MEASURED`**: An authentic numerical measurement recorded by flight instrumentation during an orbital experiment (e.g., thermocouple temperature logs from Saffire-I `PSI-98`, droplet diameter decay rates from FLEX `PSI-69`).
- **`REPORTED`**: Stated in an official NASA investigation record, Technical Memorandum (TM), or peer-reviewed flight report (e.g., fuel dimensions, cabin ambient conditions, mission dates).
- **`DERIVED`**: Deterministically calculated from identified measurements using standard physical laws (e.g., computing oxygen partial pressure $p\text{O}_2 = P_{\text{total}} \times Y_{\text{O}_2}$).
- **`MODELLED`**: Generated by a documented theoretical equation, empirical correlation, or simulation model (e.g., classical De Ris thermal balance predictions). Must clearly state assumptions and non-calibrated limitations.
- **`ILLUSTRATIVE`**: Visual or educational representation designed to communicate physical morphology (e.g., Three.js 3D meshes, HTML5 particle animations). Explicitly NOT a Navier-Stokes Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) calculation.
- **`UNKNOWN`**: Conditions where no reliable flight or ground experimental data exists in the public domain (e.g., flame propagation across electrostatic lunar regolith dust mixtures).

## 2. Partial-Gravity Combustion Science: The Evidence Gap

### 2.1. The Critical Distinction: Microgravity vs. Partial Gravity
A primary finding highlighted by the NASA Space Apps Challenge is the **severe lack of long-duration partial-gravity combustion data in NASA PSI**:
- **Microgravity ($\sim 10^{-6}\text{g}$):** Comprehensively investigated on the Space Shuttle and ISS (CIR and MSG facilities). In this regime, buoyant natural convection is suppressed, and transport is dominated by pure molecular diffusion and forced ventilation drafts ($10 - 20\ \text{cm/s}$).
- **Partial Gravity ($0.166\text{g}$ Moon, $0.38\text{g}$ Mars):** Almost zero archived flight data exists in PSI. Parabolic flights (KC-135 / Zero-G aircraft) provide only $\sim 20$ seconds of low-quality, g-jittered partial gravity. Long-duration surface fire behavior in lunar or martian exploration atmospheres remains an active, unverified frontier.

### 2.2. Correction: CFI-G vs. E-FIELD
- **ACME CFI-G (`PSI-159` — Cool Flames Investigation with Gases):**
  - **Classification:** `SUPPORTED FLIGHT RECORD [NASA PSI]`
  - Tested **gaseous alkane fuels** (ethane, propane, n-butane) inside the CIR in **microgravity ($\sim 10^{-6}\text{g}$)**.
  - Investigated low-temperature chemical reaction kinetics without buoyancy.
  - **Correction:** CFI-G did **NOT** measure lunar gravity or solid material flame spread.
- **ACME E-FIELD (`PSI-22` — Electric-Field Effects on Coflow Flames):**
  - **Classification:** `SUPPORTED FLIGHT RECORD [NASA PSI]`
  - Tested gaseous diffusion flames in microgravity inside the CIR while applying high-voltage DC electric fields (up to $10\ \text{kV}$).
  - Chemi-ions produced in the reaction zone experienced electrostatic body forces ($F = qE$), inducing an artificial ion wind that mimicked aspects of buoyant convective acceleration.
  - **Correction:** E-FIELD used electric fields as an experimental laboratory analogue to simulate convective body forces. It is **NOT** an actual lunar or martian gravitational flight test.

### 2.3. Handling Planned Investigations (FM2 & LUCI)
- **FM2 (Flammability of Materials on the Moon):**
  - Planned experimental payload in development by NASA Glenn Research Center for future Artemis Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS) landers.
  - **Classification:** `EVIDENCE GAP / PLANNED FLIGHT PAYLOAD`
  - Zero datasets exist in NASA PSI. It must **never** be labeled with a green 'Flight Data' checkmark or presented as a completed archive.
- **LUCI (Lunar Combustion Investigation):**
  - Conceptual research proposal for lunar surface combustion chambers.
  - **Classification:** `EVIDENCE GAP / CONCEPTUAL PROPOSAL`

## 3. Atmospheric Standards: NASA-STD-3001 Compliance

Claims regarding human hypoxia and cabin atmospheric limits must cite specific normative requirements rather than sweeping generalizations:

### 3.1. Normative Standard Reference
- **Standard:** NASA-STD-3001, Volume 2, Revision D: *NASA Space Flight Human-System Standard: Human Factors, Habitability, and Environmental Health*.
- **Section:** 6.1 (Internal Atmosphere).
- **Requirement `[V2 6004]` (Nominal Atmosphere Total Pressure):**
  - Cabin total pressure must be controlled within designated mission parameters (ISS nominal: $101.3\ \text{kPa}$ / $14.7\ \text{psia}$).
  - Exploration Atmosphere (Artemis EAM): $56.5\ \text{kPa}$ ($8.2\ \text{psia}$) per NASA/TP-2013-217380.
- **Requirement `[V2 6005]` (Oxygen Concentration and Partial Pressure):**
  - **Nominal Operational Boundary:** Oxygen partial pressure ($p\text{O}_2$) shall be maintained between **$19.0\ \text{kPa}$ ($2.76\ \text{psi}$)** and **$23.7\ \text{kPa}$ ($3.44\ \text{psi}$)**.
  - **Contingency Decompression Floor:** During contingency operations, $p\text{O}_2$ shall not fall below **$16.0\ \text{kPa}$ ($2.32\ \text{psi}$)** to avoid acute hypoxia.
- **Context on the $2.45\ \text{psi}$ ($16.9\ \text{kPa}$) Value:**
  - $2.45\ \text{psi}$ represents the standard aviation physiological limit (equivalent to an altitude of $3,000\ \text{m}$ / $10,000\ \text{ft}$).
  - It is **NOT** the nominal NASA spacecraft requirement.
  - **Correction:** In PYRO-SI, human health checks shall evaluate against **NASA-STD-3001 `[V2 6005]` Nominal Floor ($2.76\ \text{psi}$)** and **Contingency Floor ($2.32\ \text{psi}$)**.

## 4. Flammability Modeling & Theoretical Grounding

### 4.1. Removal of Fabricated Model Claims
The previously claimed "SIBAL empirical flame-spread equation" and the asserted "$\pm 14\%$ uncertainty bound" have been **permanently excised** from the accepted scientific requirements. 

### 4.2. Grounded Theoretical Framework: De Ris Heat Balance
Any flame spread calculations in the Flammability Simulator are designated as **`MODELLED (EXPLORATORY THEORETICAL PROXY)`** based on classical thin-fuel thermal energy balance (De Ris, 1969):
$$V_f \approx \frac{\sqrt{2} \cdot k_g \cdot (T_f - T_v)}{\rho_s \cdot c_s \cdot \tau \cdot (T_v - T_0)} \cdot \left(\frac{U_{\text{eff}} + U_{\text{buoy}}}{U_{\text{ref}}}\right)^{1/2}$$

Where:
- $k_g$ is gas thermal conductivity.
- $\rho_s, c_s, \tau$ are the solid fuel density, specific heat, and half-thickness.
- $T_f, T_v, T_0$ are the flame, vaporization, and initial solid temperatures.
- $U_{\text{eff}}$ is the forced ventilation draft ($10 - 20\ \text{cm/s}$).
- $U_{\text{buoy}}$ is the buoyant convective velocity, which vanishes in microgravity ($g = 0$) and scales as $\sqrt{g \cdot L \cdot \Delta T / T_\infty}$ under non-zero gravity.
- **Scientific Limitation:** This equation provides qualitative physical scaling trends. It has not been empirically tuned to flight data within this codebase and must never be cited as an authoritative $\pm 14\%$ validated prediction.
