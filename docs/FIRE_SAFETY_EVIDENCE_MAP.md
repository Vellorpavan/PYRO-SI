## Executive Summary

The **Spacecraft Fire Safety Evidence Map** establishes an unbroken, forensic chain of custody between NASA microgravity combustion flight data archived in the Physical Sciences Informatics (PSI) repository and the operational fire safety disciplines required for future human space exploration:
1. **Fire Prevention** (Material selection, flammability test standards, atmospheric limits).
2. **Fire Detection** (Smoke particle agglomeration, optical scattering, dust discrimination).
3. **Fire Suppression** (Ventilation shutdown, nitrogen inerting, droplet quenching, depressurization).
4. **Terrestrial Spinoffs & Earth Applications** (Non-toxic water mist suppression, clean combustion, industrial aerosol monitoring).

This document serves as the normative engineering foundation for all algorithmic models, UI decision matrices, and AI research co-pilot synthesis in GODEYE // PYRO-SI.

## 2. Pillar 1: Fire Prevention

Fire prevention is the primary line of defense in crewed space missions. Once ignition occurs in an enclosed spacecraft, extinguishing the fire and scrubbing toxic combustion products consumes critical crew time and life-support consumables.

### 2.1. Material Screening and Normative Standards
- **Standard:** **NASA-STD-6001B** ("Flammability, Offgassing, and Compatibility Requirements and Test Procedures").
- **Test 1 (Upward Flame Propagation):** Standard laboratory screening test performed in normal gravity ($1.0\text{g}$) within a worst-case oxygen-enriched environment. A material passes Test 1 if flame propagation does not exceed $15\text{ cm}$ ($6\text{ inches}$) and no burning droplets ignite a lint indicator below the sample.
- **The Microgravity Inversion Paradox:** Terrestrial $1.0\text{g}$ tests impose high buoyant convective flow ($30 - 50\text{ cm/s}$), which cools the flame base. In microgravity, low-speed forced flows ($2 - 10\text{ cm/s}$) generated by habitat air circulation fans provide sufficient oxygen transport without the convective heat-sink effect of $1.0\text{g}$ buoyancy. Consequently, **certain materials that pass NASA-STD-6001 Test 1 on Earth can support sustained combustion in low-flow space environments**.

### 2.4. Atmospheric Standards Integration
- **ISS Nominal Standard:** $14.7\text{ psia}$ ($101.3\text{ kPa}$), $21.0\%\ \text{O}_2$, $pO_2 = 3.087\text{ psia}$ ($21.28\text{ kPa}$). Normoxic, safe nitrogen diluent ($78\%$), nominal flammability risks.
- **Artemis Exploration Atmosphere (EAM):** $8.2\text{ psia}$ ($56.5\text{ kPa}$), $34.0\%\ \text{O}_2$, $pO_2 = 2.788\text{ psia}$ ($19.22\text{ kPa}$). Reduces pre-breathe times for EVA spacewalks. **Critical Fire Risk:** Although $pO_2$ is normoxic, the oxygen mole fraction ($34\%$) drastically reduces the protective heat-capacity buffer of inert nitrogen, lowering material ignition energies and accelerating flame spread rates by $30 - 50\%$.
- **NASA-STD-3001 Compliance Limits:**
  - `[V2 6002]`: Minimum $30\%$ inert diluent gas ($\text{N}_2$) for missions $>2$ weeks.
  - `[V2 6003]`: Inspired oxygen partial pressure range ($18.5 - 20.9\text{ kPa}$ / $2.68 - 3.03\text{ psia}$).
  - `[V2 6006]`: Total cabin pressure limits ($34.5 - 103\text{ kPa}$ / $5.0 - 15.0\text{ psia}$).

## 3. Pillar 2: Fire Detection

In terrestrial buildings, buoyant plumes carry hot smoke upward to ceiling-mounted ionization or optical smoke detectors. In microgravity, **buoyant plumes do not exist**. Smoke disperses solely via forced air circulation loops (HVAC) and molecular/Brownian diffusion.

### 3.1. Smoke Particle Agglomeration Physics: SAME-R (`PSI-101`)
- **Key Scientific Finding:** On Earth, buoyant convection rapidly clears smoke particles away from the reaction zone in fractions of a second, resulting in small primary particles ($d_p < 0.1\ \mu\text{m}$). In microgravity, the absence of buoyancy causes smoke particles to linger in high-temperature zones for seconds to minutes.
- **Particle Sizing Results:** Measurements using the Differential Mobility Analyzer (DMA) and Condensation Particle Counter (CPC) during the Smoke Aerosol Measurement Experiment - Reflight (SAME-R) proved that microgravity smoke particles coagulate into large, chain-like agglomerates with geometric mean diameters of **$0.5 - 2.5\ \mu\text{m}$** (over an order of magnitude larger than terrestrial equivalents).
- **Detector Design Implication:** Standard terrestrial ionization detectors (which detect sub-micron ions and particles) exhibit degraded sensitivity in microgravity. Spacecraft smoke alarms must prioritize **forward-scattering optical nephelometers** tuned to the $0.5 - 2.5\ \mu\text{m}$ size band.

### 3.2. Dust vs. Smoke Discrimination: DAFT (`PSI-47`)
- **Test Campaign:** Dust and Aerosol Measurement Feasibility Test (DAFT) aboard ISS Express Rack 4.
- **Instrument Evaluated:** Modified TSI P-Trak condensation nuclei counter equipped with a specialized microgravity capillary wick for alcohol condensation. Evaluated against Arizona Road Dust (ARD Fine/Ultrafine) dispersed in $15\text{ L}$ Mylar test volumes.
- **Operational Challenge:** In Lunar and Martian habitats, abrasive planetary dust will inevitably be tracked inside the habitat on EVA spacesuits. Mineral dust particles ($1 - 10\ \mu\text{m}$) overlap in optical scattering cross-section with large microgravity smoke agglomerates, risking catastrophic false alarms or masked real fire incidents.
- **Architectural Solution:** DAFT demonstrated that condensation nuclei counters and multi-angle optical scattering can discriminate between spherical mineral dust grains and fractal, carbonaceous soot agglomerates, establishing the sensor baseline for Orion, Lunar Gateway, and Mars Transit vehicles.

### 3.3. Soot Formation and Smoke Points: SPICE (`PSI-107`)
- **Investigation:** Smoke Point In Co-flow Experiment (SPICE) in the Microgravity Science Glovebox.
- **Observation:** In microgravity, the fuel flow rate required to produce soot escape (the "smoke point") is **significantly lower** than in normal gravity. Longer residence times allow polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) to nucleate and form soot precursors even in small, low-velocity flames.
- **Implication:** Early fire detection algorithms can monitor trace gas-phase soot precursors and carbon monoxide ($CO$) at much earlier stages of overheating before flaming combustion erupts.

## 4. Pillar 3: Fire Suppression

Fire suppression in a spacecraft cannot utilize water sprinkler deluge (which causes electrical shorts, free-floating water hazards, and cabin drownings) or toxic Halon agents (which cannot be easily scrubbed by the Environmental Control and Life Support System [ECLSS]).

### 4.1. Ventilation Shutdown as Primary Tactical Response
- **Evidence Base (BASS-II / PSI-25):** 
  - BASS-II proved that solid flames in zero gravity require forced airflow to survive. At velocities below $2 - 5\text{ cm/s}$, heat loss from radiation exceeds the chemical heat release from diffusion-limited oxygen transport, driving the flame to extinction.
  - **Operational Procedure:** The immediate first action upon smoke alarm confirmation is **emergency ventilation shutdown (inter-module isolation valves and cabin circulation fans)**. Depriving the fire of forced oxidizer drift will cause small flames to self-extinguish or shrink to manageable quiescent embers.

### 4.2. Gaseous Dilution and Inerting
- **Evidence Base (BASS-II & ACME BRE):**
  - When ventilation shutdown alone is insufficient (e.g., in localized high-oxygen pockets or with thick fuels), gaseous nitrogen ($\text{N}_2$) dilution is applied.
  - In BASS-II, reducing the ambient oxygen concentration below $14 - 15\%\ \text{O}_2$ via nitrogen introduction reliably achieved flame extinguishment across all solid PMMA and fabric samples.
  - *Distinction Note:* BASS-II investigated gaseous nitrogen dilution inerting, not liquid $\text{CO}_2$ extinguisher bottles.

### 4.3. Liquid Droplet Extinction and Cool Flame Hazards: FLEX (`PSI-69`)
- **Investigation:** Flame Extinguishment Experiment (FLEX) in the Multi-user Droplet Combustion Apparatus (MDCA).
- **Extinction Boundary Mapping:** Mapped limiting oxygen concentrations and inert suppressant mole fractions (using $\text{CO}_2$, Helium, and $\text{SF}_6$) for liquid hydrocarbon droplet extinction.
- **Cool Flame Discovery:** Discovered that after apparent hot flame extinction ($T \approx 1700\text{ K}$), liquid droplets can enter a second stage of low-temperature combustion ($T \approx 700\text{ K}$) driven by alkylperoxy radical chemistry. These "cool flames" emit virtually no visible light and minimal heat, but can persist invisibly and reignite into dangerous hot flames if fresh oxygen-rich air is introduced.
- **Suppression Protocol:** Fire suppression systems must continue monitoring and inerting the fire zone long after visible flames vanish, preventing cool-flame re-ignition during post-fire ventilation restoration.

### 4.4. Electrostatic Flame Control: ACME E-FIELD (`PSI-22`)
- **Investigation:** Electric-Field Effects on Coflow Flames (E-FIELD).
- **Physical Mechanism:** Hydrocarbon flames produce chemi-ions ($\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$, $\text{CHO}^+$). Applying high-voltage DC/AC electric fields ($5 - 10\text{ kV}$) exerts a Lorentz-like Coulomb body force ($F = qE$) on these ions, creating an artificial ionic wind.
- **Future Spacecraft Applications:** E-FIELD demonstrated that targeted electrostatic fields can deflect flame tips away from delicate electronic enclosures, destabilize flame anchors, and achieve electrical flame quenching without discharging chemical extinguishing mass.

### 4.5. Emergency Cabin Depressurization: SAFFIRE-I (`PSI-98`)
- **Investigation:** Spacecraft Fire Safety Experiment (SAFFIRE-I) aboard Cygnus OA-6.
- **Procedure:** Following large-scale material combustion ($0.94\text{ m}$ sample), Cygnus executed automated cabin depressurization to space vacuum.
- **Physical Dynamics:** SAFFIRE-I provided empirical verification of cabin pressure decay, cooling rates, and flame extinction dynamics during rapid venting. Depressurization to vacuum ($P < 0.1\text{ psia}$) remains the ultimate fail-safe suppression method for uncrewed compartments or isolated space habitat modules.

### 5.1. Fine Water Mist (FWM) Extinguisher Development
- **Origin:** Following the Montreal Protocol banning ozone-depleting Halon 1301 and Halon 1211, NASA Glenn Research Center collaborated with ADA Technologies, Inc., and the Colorado School of Mines to develop a clean, non-toxic fire extinguisher for the International Space Station.
- **Physics Mechanism:** Utilizing micro-droplet vaporization kinetics studied in NASA droplet combustion experiments (FLEX / MDCA), the fine water mist system discharges high-pressure water micro-droplets ($10 - 50\ \mu\text{m}$) accelerated by a nitrogen propellant. The ultra-fine mist evaporates instantly upon approaching the flame base, extracting enormous latent heat of vaporization ($2260\text{ J/g}$) and expanding into steam ($1700\times$ volume) to dilute oxygen locally without creating pooling water puddles.
- **Commercial Spinoff:** ADA Technologies commercialized the technology for terrestrial use. Today, fine water mist systems protect commercial airliner passenger cabins, naval shipboard engine rooms, telecommunication centers, and irreplaceable historical libraries where water deluge would cause catastrophic property destruction.

### 5.2. Dust-Discriminating Aerosol Detectors
- **Origin:** Data from SAME-R (`PSI-101`) and DAFT (`PSI-47`) on particle size distributions and dust interference in microgravity directly contributed to new algorithms for terrestrial optical smoke alarms.
- **Commercial Spinoff:** Dual-wavelength (Blue and Infrared LED) smoke detectors now utilize polarization and scattering ratio algorithms developed for space exploration to distinguish dangerous combustion soot from nuisance dust, cooking aerosols, and steam in domestic and industrial environments, drastically reducing false emergency dispatches.

### 5.3. Low-Temperature Clean Combustion (HCCI Engines)
- **Origin:** The discovery of cool flames in spherical microgravity droplets (FLEX) and gaseous coflow flames (CFI-G) provided combustion scientists with isolated, unperturbed datasets of low-temperature hydrocarbon oxidation kinetics ($500 - 800\text{ K}$).
- **Commercial Spinoff:** These kinetic mechanisms have been directly incorporated into kinetic modeling software used by automotive and aerospace engineers to design Homogeneous Charge Compression Ignition (HCCI) engines. HCCI engines operate at lower peak combustion temperatures, virtually eliminating thermal $\text{NO}_x$ emissions while dramatically increasing thermodynamic fuel efficiency.

## 7. Operational Recommendations for Extraterrestrial Habitats

1. **HVAC Airflow Management:** Spacecraft and lunar habitat HVAC design must eliminate stagnant pockets while avoiding forced air velocities $>15\text{ cm/s}$ adjacent to solid polymer conduits or equipment bays.
2. **Atmospheric Optimization:** If operating under Exploration Atmosphere conditions ($34\%\ \text{O}_2$, $8.2\text{ psia}$), material flammability certification must be strictly re-evaluated at $34\%\ \text{O}_2$. Materials approved under $21\%\ \text{O}_2$ sea-level standards cannot be assumed fire-safe in EAM.
3. **Dual-Wavelength Optical Detection:** All habitat smoke detection channels must combine forward-scattering blue ($470\text{ nm}$) and infrared ($850\text{ nm}$) optical sensors to discriminate large microgravity smoke agglomerates ($>0.5\ \mu\text{m}$) from abrasive lunar regolith dust particles.
4. **Automated Two-Stage Suppression:**
   - *Stage 1:* Immediate automated ventilation isolation to starve combustion of convective oxidizer flow.
   - *Stage 2:* Targeted fine water mist or localized nitrogen gas flooding, followed by persistent thermal imaging to monitor for non-luminous cool flames prior to module re-entry.
